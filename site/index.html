How it works

Zero change to how you drive.

An adapter never opens the Bluetooth link on its own, so AutoSentry keeps a quiet background monitor that does it for you. Nobody has to open the app or touch the tablet.

  1. Low power

    Truck off

    The adapter sleeps. The tablet wakes every 15 seconds for one quick key-on check: no GPS, no wake lock.

  2. Full power

    Key on

    Before you crank, a key-on check snapshots battery voltage, readings and trouble codes. Then it times the crank.

  3. Full power

    Driving

    Four readings a second plus GPS. A dropped link is retried at once and the gap is filled from GPS.

  4. Low power

    Shut off

    Two minutes after the engine stops, the trip closes and is logged with its distance and engine time.

Features

Everything the truck will tell you, on one screen.

Pick your gauges

About 35 readings, including load, EGR, torque, injection timing and the 7.3L’s Ford ICP and IPR. Out-of-range gauges get an amber or red border.

Oil temp, not a guess

On the 7.3L the temperature that matters is engine oil, read from Ford’s enhanced PID instead of assuming coolant.

Key-on check

Every key cycle: battery voltage, readings and stored and pending codes before cranking, then crank time, tries and lowest cranking voltage.

Oil life that moves

Counts down from real miles on the 3,000 mi severe / 5,000 mi normal schedule. Idling, engine hours and oil temp flag severe duty.

Service reminders

Fuel and air filters, transmission, transfer case, diffs, coolant, grease points and age-based wear items, flagged approaching, due or overdue.

Trip computer

Speed, distance and engine hours, with every trip logged automatically. MPG on the 7.3L comes once Ford’s fuel-quantity reading is verified on a real truck.

Trouble codes

Stored, pending and permanent codes, read and decoded. A connection test names the exact step that fails when something won’t talk.

Service log with proof

Log the work and attach receipts and part photos. Each record is labeled by the evidence behind it.

Honest data

It tells you what it knows, and what it doesn’t.

Dropped connections, missing readings and stale values are shown as they are, never filled in with simulated data. Every number says where it came from.

  • MeasuredReturned by the truck, with a timestamp and trip.
  • RecordedService you entered, with an optional receipt or photo.
  • CalculatedMileage, engine hours and oil life worked out from stored data.
  • RecommendedRaised only when a documented threshold is near.
  • UnavailableThe truck didn’t answer, and the app says so.

Privacy

Your truck’s data stays on your device.

  • No account and no cloud. The app doesn’t even ask for internet access.
  • Trips, readings and service records are stored on the device, and Android backup is turned off so they aren’t copied anywhere else.
  • Reports and the debug log leave the device only when you tap share.

Install

Three steps to your dash.

What you need

  • An Android 7.0 or newer phone or tablet.
  • An ELM327-compatible OBD-II adapter that uses classic Bluetooth. Bluetooth LE-only adapters (like the OBDLink CX) won’t connect.
    OBDLink MX+ (recommended) OBDLink LX

    As an Amazon Associate AutoSentry earns from qualifying purchases.

  • An OBD-II vehicle. Readings and the service schedule are tuned for the 2000 F-250 7.3L Power Stroke; other vehicles get the standard readings they answer.

For the 7.3L’s Ford readings (oil temp, ICP, IPR), the adapter must support the truck’s J1850 PWM protocol.

Install it

  1. On the phone or tablet, open the latest release and tap the AutoSentry-….apk file.
  2. When Android asks, allow your browser to install apps.
  3. Open AutoSentry. The setup wizard covers permissions, picking the paired adapter, a truck connection test, your odometer and your gauges.

Updates install over the top and keep your trips and service history. Don’t uninstall first.
